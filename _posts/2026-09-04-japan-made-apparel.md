Neither is a marketing story — both are real production constraints that show up directly in the finished product.

## 1. Loopwheel Tubular Pocket T-Shirt

**Made in Japan.** Loopwheel (tsuri-ami, 吊り編み) knitting is a nearly extinct technique — a circular knitting machine that uses only gravity for tension, working so slowly it produces roughly one meter of fabric per hour. Wakayama Prefecture is the only place left in the world with working loopwheel machines, down from around ten mills in 1995 to a small handful today, Kanekichi Kogyo among them. The result is a soft, stress-free fabric that holds its shape and texture through repeated washing far better than a modern high-speed knit. Japan's fashion press — Begin magazine, Lifehacker Japan, the BEAMS blog — has covered the loopwheel revival extensively, with the Loopwheeler brand (founded 1999) widely credited with popularizing the technique domestically. When the production method itself is this scarce, the quality argument mostly makes itself.

## 2. Buzz Rickson's Zip Hoodie Sweatshirt

**Made in Japan** by Toyo Enterprise Co., the same company behind the Sugar Cane denim line covered elsewhere on this site. Buzz Rickson's has built its entire identity since 1993 around MIL-SPEC-accurate reproductions of American military garments — a level of obsessive historical detail that's earned it real distribution in Japan through Amazon.co.jp, Rakuten, and premium retailer BEAMS. This zip hoodie applies that same workwear-grade construction standard to a genuinely everyday sweatshirt, rather than a novelty military costume piece.

**Made in Japan**, same maker as above. This is a faithful reproduction of the U.S. Army Air Force Type A-2 flight jacket, right down to painted nose-art bomber styling — the kind of workwear-adjacent piece that rewards the brand's meticulous MIL-SPEC approach with a genuinely premium leather garment rather than a costume-shop imitation.

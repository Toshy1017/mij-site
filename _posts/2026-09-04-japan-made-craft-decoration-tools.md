A craft tape that turned "washi tape" into a worldwide hobby, a utility knife whose snap-off blade design is now the industry default, and the small precision-glue tool that quietly replaced messy liquid glue on Japanese desks.

## 1. mt Washi Masking Tape (Kamoi Kakoshi)

**Made in Japan**, explicitly marked Japan Import. Kamoi Kakoshi is a 100-year-old Japanese company, and its mt brand is the original washi paper masking tape — the product that essentially invented "washi tape" as a decorative craft category, rather than following an existing trend. It shows up now in scrapbooking, gift wrapping, planner decoration, even hiding LEDs and camera indicator lights. Across mt's many colorways on Amazon.co.jp, ratings run a consistent 4.4 to 4.5 stars over anywhere from roughly 190 to more than 1,000 reviews per SKU, with one variant ranking as a #1 best seller. The recurring praise: real adhesive strength that still peels away clean, without residue or paper damage.

## 2. Olfa L-1 Utility Knife

**Made in Japan.** Olfa, founded in Osaka in 1956, invented the snap-off blade utility knife — the segmented blade you break off a fresh edge from instead of resharpening or replacing the whole blade, a design so good it's now the global default rather than a Japanese specialty. This L-1 model uses Japanese tool steel with a ratchet-lock mechanism for adjustable cutting depth, built for repeated heavy-duty use rather than as a disposable box cutter. Japanese reviewers of Olfa's L-type cutter line describe it as a tool "chosen and used by many technicians over many years," with spot-checked ratings running as high as 4.8 out of 5 — the kind of quiet, durable reputation a tool earns only through decades of professional use.

## 3. Kokuyo Dotliner Tape Glue

**Made in Japan.** Kokuyo's Dotliner replaced messy liquid glue and glue sticks with a tape-format applicator that lays down a precise line of dot adhesive exactly where you want it — a small mechanical idea that turned out to matter a lot for anyone doing regular paper craft or office work. It's a more modest pick on this list than the other two: ratings across the Dotliner family run 3.8 to 4.1 stars on Amazon.co.jp, solid rather than spectacular, with the main recurring complaint being refill cost over time rather than the adhesive performance itself. Reviewers consistently prefer the precision and lack of mess over liquid glue, which is the actual job it's designed to do.

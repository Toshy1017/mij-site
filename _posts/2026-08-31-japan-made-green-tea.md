Not every "Japanese green tea" on Amazon is actually processed and packaged in Japan — some historic Japanese tea brands blend and package their US-market tea domestically in America, using Japan-grown leaf. For this list, we only kept teas where the actual tea-making step (not just the raw leaf) happens in Japan.

*Our assessments below are M.I.J's own conclusions, formed by analyzing patterns across a large number of customer reviews — not firsthand tasting. Prices change constantly and aren't reliably readable by our research process — check the current price on the product page itself before buying.*

## 1. SUGIMOTO TEA (Hojicha / Sencha / Matcha / Genmaicha)

**Made in Japan, Shizuoka** — stated directly and consistently across every SUGIMOTO TEA listing on Amazon.

The company, 杉本製茶株式会社 (Sugimoto Tea Company Inc.), was founded in 1946 in Shimada City, Shizuoka Prefecture — one of Japan's premier tea-growing regions — and opened a Seattle office in 2005 purely to handle export and distribution; the tea itself is processed in Japan, using a heavier roasting/steaming style the company is known for that produces a bolder, fuller-bodied cup than lighter sencha styles. That shows in how it drinks: this is not a delicate, typical bagged green tea — the Sencha leans closer to the body of a black tea, which makes it a good pick for people who've bounced off lighter green teas before. The Hojicha (roasted tea) is the standout of the lineup, with a warm, comforting character carrying notes of toasted rice and seaweed alongside a strong umami backbone. We couldn't confirm this brand's presence on Amazon.co.jp in this research pass, so we don't have Japan-side consumer review data to compare against — the manufacturing-origin claim stands on its own regardless.

## 2. Ocha & Co. Organic Sencha

**Made in Japan.** The company sources leaf grown at 400m elevation in Shizuoka's Southern Alps, and the tea is blended by a winner of Japan's Ministry of Agriculture Tea Competition — an actual Japan-side processing step, not just US repackaging of raw leaf. A US warehouse handles shipping only.

This is the strongest all-around recommendation on this list. The reputation here is unusually consistent: buyers who've tried tea from many different companies repeatedly single this one out as the best they've found, and the clearest signal of quality is behavioral, not just anecdotal — people run out and immediately reorder rather than switching to something else. Expect a golden-green cup, mild bitterness, low astringency, and a pronounced umami character. We didn't turn up any notable negative reviews for this product in our research.

## 3. Uji Matcha Kyo-no-hana (Ujitawara Seichajo Co., Ltd.)

**Made in Japan** — the Amazon listing names the manufacturer directly: Ujitawara Seichajo Co., Ltd. This is a genuinely cross-market product: the identical 京の花 (Kyo-no-hana) matcha is sold by the same manufacturer directly on Amazon.co.jp in Japan.

We couldn't find review data specific to this exact matcha product, but the manufacturer as a whole has an exceptionally strong track record in Japan — its storefront holds **4.82 out of 5 stars across 3,411 reviews** on Rakuten Japan, with buyers consistently praising fast, careful shipping and packaging on top of product quality. That's a shop-wide rating rather than a per-product one, so treat it as a strong general signal of trustworthiness in this specific manufacturer rather than a rating of this exact matcha.

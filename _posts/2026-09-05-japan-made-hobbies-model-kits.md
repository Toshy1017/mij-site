This one turned up a bigger finding than a product list: two of the best-known Japanese hobby exports — Gunpla plastic model kits and anime figures like figma and Nendoroid — are Japanese-*designed* and Japanese-*branded*, but largely aren't Japanese-*manufactured* anymore. We'd rather tell you that plainly than sell you a "Japan-made" Gundam kit that doesn't hold up to the label.

*Our assessments below are M.I.J's own conclusions, formed by analyzing patterns across a large number of customer reviews — not firsthand use.*

## Why Gunpla and anime figures didn't make this list

**Anime figures (figma, Nendoroid, and other Good Smile Company scale figures) are made in China.** Good Smile Company is a real, major Japanese hobby company, but actual production runs through Good Smile Arts Shanghai, its Chinese manufacturing subsidiary established in 2018. This is treated as settled fact in hobbyist circles, and it checked out on the Amazon.com listings we looked at — not one stated a country of manufacture, which is itself consistent with a brand that doesn't want to lead with "Made in China" on a Japanese IP product.

**Gunpla is manufactured in a mix of Japan and China, with no way to tell which from the Amazon.com listing.** Bandai Hobby's own "From the Manufacturer" copy on Amazon.com is careful to say "Designed in Japan" — not "Made in Japan" — and that phrasing tracks reality: production runs through both Bandai's Japan factory and Chinese lines, and individual Amazon.com kit listings don't say which one made your specific box. We're not willing to slap a "Made in Japan" label on a coin flip.

**Tamiya's plastic model kits didn't make it for the same reason.** Tamiya is a legitimate, respected Japanese company, but most of its plastic kits are manufactured at Tamiya's Philippines factory, and the Amazon.com listing we checked didn't state a country of manufacture at all.

## What's actually left — and it's still worth your time

## 1. Karakuri Creation Group Egg Puzzle Box

**Made in Japan**, and specifically credited to a named craftsman — the listing states "Handmade in Japan by Akio Kamei." Kamei is a real, well-documented artist in the Hakone-region karakuri puzzle-box tradition. Reviews are few (4 ratings) but unanimous 5 stars, including one from a buyer whose experienced-woodworker father needed real effort to solve it.

## 2. Karakuri Small Box #3

**Made in Japan** — Karakuri Creation Group is a genuine Hakone-region puzzle-box maker collective, and the listing's own care instructions are headed "Care and Upkeep of Japanese Wooden Puzzle Boxes." Slightly less explicit than the egg puzzle above (no single craftsman credited), but consistent with the same verified maker. Reviews are a very small sample (2 ratings) but both 5 stars.

[Check current price on Amazon →](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B004105DI4?tag=yamanet1017-20)

## 3. Pokémon Card Game Mega Symphonia Booster Box (Japanese, 30 Packs)

**Made in Japan** — this is explicitly the Japanese-market product, not a US reprint. Japanese-language Pokémon TCG cards are printed in Japan by Creatures Inc./The Pokémon Company, a genuinely separate print run from the English-market version. Amazon.com backs this up directly and strongly: 4.5 out of 5 across 1,324 ratings, with reviewers repeatedly confirming the boxes arrive sealed and unresealed with legitimate pull rates. A few note the price runs 15–20% above grey-market/scalper pricing — you're paying for Amazon Prime shipping and buyer protection on an import, not getting a discount.

[Check current price on Amazon →](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0F189YH8W?tag=yamanet1017-20)

## 4. Yu-Gi-Oh! OCG Duel Monsters Code of the Duelist Box (Japanese)

**Made in Japan** — the genuine Japanese OCG (Official Card Game) product, printed and sold in Japan by Konami of Japan, distinct from the English TCG version. The listing's own reviews from Japan (not a separate cross-reference search — actual reviews on this Amazon.com listing) confirm this is the real domestic product, with buyers specifically noting the absence of "searching" (a scalper practice of feeling for rare cards through unopened packs) and good pull rates. Currently listed as unavailable on Amazon.com with no restock date given.

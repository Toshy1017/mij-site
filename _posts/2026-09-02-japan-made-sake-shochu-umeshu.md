## 1. Dassai 45 Junmai Daiginjo

Dassai has become the sake brand most responsible for introducing premium sake to people outside Japan, and 45 is the entry point into their entire range — the number refers to rice polished down to 45% of its original size, the defining metric for premium sake quality. Expect a fruity, floral, approachable profile that doesn't demand any prior sake knowledge to enjoy; it's smoother and more forgiving than the sharper, more traditional styles that can intimidate newcomers. If you've only ever had sake at a sushi restaurant, this is the bottle that shows you what it can actually be.

## 2. Choya Wine Umeshu

Choya is the #1 umeshu brand in Japan, and this is the classic version — whole ume plums steeped for months to produce a liqueur that's sweet-tart with a distinct almond note from the pit. It's genuinely one of the most versatile Japanese drinks to keep on hand: sip it chilled or on the rocks, or use it as a mixer, and it works in all three settings without needing any special expertise. This is the easiest, most approachable bottle on this entire list for someone who's never tried a Japanese spirit before.

[View on Amazon →](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B006GIPJQA?tag=yamanet1017-20)

## 3. Iichiko Frasco Barley Shochu

Iichiko essentially defined modern barley shochu, and Frasco sits at the top of their range — made from 100% barley koji, with a clean aroma and a body that's noticeably richer than the paper-pack everyday versions. This is the style behind Japan's shochu highball trend, and it's easy to see why once you taste it: it's mellow enough to drink neat but has enough character to hold its own mixed with soda. If whisky highballs are already your thing, this is the natural next bottle to explore.

## 1. Yamazaki 12 Year Single Malt

Suntory's flagship single malt, and the bottle that put Japanese whisky on the world map after taking "World's Best Single Malt" at international competition. Expect a genuinely layered dram — peach, pineapple, and grapefruit up front, Mizunara (Japanese oak) giving it a spice and incense note nothing else quite replicates, rounding into coconut, cranberry, and a long ginger-cinnamon finish. This is the single bottle to reach for if you want to understand what all the fuss over Japanese whisky is actually about.

## 2. Nikka Whisky Single Malt Yoichi

Made exclusively at Nikka's Yoichi distillery on Hokkaido's northern coast, using coal-fired pot stills — a genuinely old-school production method most distilleries abandoned decades ago. That method, combined with the coastal Hokkaido air, gives Yoichi a distinctive smoky, briny character that's closer to an Islay Scotch than the fruit-forward style Yamazaki is known for. If Yamazaki is Japanese whisky's polished ambassador, Yoichi is its rugged, uncompromising counterpart — a genuinely different experience worth having if you only know the former.

[View on Amazon →](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B072534K8N?tag=yamanet1017-20)

## 3. Roku Japanese Craft Gin

Six Japanese botanicals — including sakura (cherry blossom), sencha green tea, and sansho pepper — infused separately and blended, which is a meaningfully different (and more labor-intensive) process than most gins use. The result is a genuinely distinct profile: floral and clean up front from the cherry blossom, with an earthy green-tea undertone that sets it apart from the juniper-forward gins most people default to. This is the pick if you want a gin and tonic that actually tastes like somewhere specific rather than a generic botanical blend.
